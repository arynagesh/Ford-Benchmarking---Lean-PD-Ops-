Tesla

Disruptor
Strongest, most independently-evidenced company in the set — but not a clean sweep: genuinely Emerging on Modularity given a narrowing model lineup.

Financial context (not a rubric-scored construct)

NIO's cumulative net losses exceeded RMB 120 billion through mid-2025. The company posted its first-ever quarterly GAAP profit in Q4 2025 (RMB 282.7M) after 11 years, then slipped back to a narrow net loss in Q1 2026 (RMB 48.1M) while remaining profitable on an adjusted basis. This is real and important context on NIO's overall commercial sustainability — but none of the 9 rubric metrics score corporate financial health, only PD/PDO capability. Noted here so the ratings above aren't mistaken for a signal that NIO is a thriving business overall.

Ford

Baseline pending
No evidence cards for Ford. This is deliberate.

Why there's nothing to click through to here

Every other company on this page is scored from external, publicly available evidence. Ford is the subject of the benchmarking exercise, not a comparator — its row will be populated by the Step-5 internal baseline, run against this same locked rubric, not by web research. The heat-map's "exp:" hints are the rubric's stated expectations, not findings, and none of them will convert to a rated cell until that baseline exists. Publishing placeholder Ford evidence here would blur exactly the line this project is built to protect — no internal figure gets quoted before it's actually measured.

Methodology note: this pass added eight genuinely new findings from targeted research on Configuration Mgmt/Digital Thread, OTA, Integrated Services, and Feature-Driven Definition — the columns with the least coverage. Toyota gained a real but narrowly-scoped PLM case (Emerging) and a second Lagging rating on OTA specifically. VW gained a Strong Config Mgmt rating from a real but dated (2008) enterprise Teamcenter deployment, plus an Emerging Feature-Driven Definition rating from its real, current SOA architecture investment. Hyundai gained a clean Lagging rating on Integrated Services — not from lacking capability, but from a documented decision to remove subscription fees rather than monetize them. BMW gained a mixed Emerging rating on Integrated Services (real revenue, but a very public monetization failure) and a Strong rating on Feature-Driven Definition, since Functions on Demand is a real architectural principle independent of that one commercial misstep. NIO gained a Strong Feature-Driven Definition rating from its Battery-as-a-Service model — six years old, independently reported, and arguably more direct evidence for this construct than Tesla's. BYD's Configuration Mgmt/Digital Thread evidence, Toyota/VW/BYD's remaining Integrated Services cells, and Toyota/Hyundai's Feature-Driven Definition were searched and left blank — no usable source was found, and none was invented to fill the gap. Every point was verified via web search while building this page. Points repeating the same underlying source across multiple cells are labeled, so repetition isn't mistaken for independent corroboration.
