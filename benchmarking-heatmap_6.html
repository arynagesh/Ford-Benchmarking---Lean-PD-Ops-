What the pattern says

Two diagonal blocks, with real texture in both. Disruptors (Tesla, BYD, NIO) concentrate in Cluster B — but Tesla is Emerging, not Leader, on Modularity: its lineup narrowed to three models in 2026 (S and X discontinued), missing the 3-row SUV and midsize-truck segments outright, so cross-segment interchangeability can't be demonstrated at the scale legacy platforms show. That's a scope choice, not a failure — but it's a real gap, not a blank.

Legacy OEMs split on software, and split again on monetization. Toyota and VW are genuinely Lagging on SDV Maturity & HW–SW Integration — independently reported, not estimated: VW's Cariad unit posted over $7.5B in combined losses (2023–24) and paid Rivian $5.8B for software access; Toyota shipped its first meaningful OTA update only with the 2026 RAV4, a gap real enough to also rate it Lagging on OTA specifically. Hyundai sits at Emerging on the same architecture metric — real ambition, not yet independently verified — but is Lagging on Integrated Services for a different reason entirely: it actively removed nearly all Bluelink subscription fees in 2023, standardizing free access rather than build a revenue line. BMW's Integrated Services rating is Emerging for the opposite reason — a real monetization attempt (heated-seat subscriptions) that triggered public backlash and had to be walked back, leaving a narrower surviving service tier.

Config Mgmt & Digital Thread is no longer NIO/BMW-only. Toyota ran a real, if narrowly-scoped, PLM digital-thread pilot (one engineering division); VW deployed Siemens Teamcenter enterprise-wide across 45,000 users — though that source dates to 2008, flagged accordingly. BYD's system remains genuinely unfound, left blank rather than guessed.

Feature-Driven Definition gained real texture too. NIO's Battery as a Service (since 2020, now extended to flexible daily/monthly battery upgrades via its Onvo sub-brand) decouples a core vehicle capability into an on-demand feature — arguably cleaner evidence for this metric than Tesla's. BMW's Functions on Demand is real architecture, not just a monetization tactic (unify hardware, differentiate via software license) — rated Strong despite the well-known heated-seat backlash, since the underlying architecture principle held even where one feature's pricing didn't. VW is rated Emerging: a genuine €5.6B investment in service-oriented, feature-decoupled architecture (Cariad's E³ 2.0, via Elektrobit), but not yet a demonstrated governing feature catalog.

Read the confidence dots, not just the colors. All five Lagging ratings are 🟢 — independently reported or drawn from the company's own on-the-record retreat, not analyst estimates. Legacy Cluster-A strengths lean 🟡 (mgmt-disclosed platform metrics). The evidence base is uneven by construct, not by company — corroborate 🟡 cells before they anchor a recommendation.
